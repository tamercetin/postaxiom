Development path

Start narrow. Prove decision value. Expand deliberately.

The company should begin with a clear commercial wedge, demonstrate measurable decision value, and only then broaden toward a more general enterprise decision layer.

Phase 1

Forecast + pricing intelligence

Launch around demand, revenue, pricing, and promotion use cases for large multi-series businesses in the U.S. market.

  • Forecast Engine v1
  • Pricing and promotion analytics
  • Scenario memos for demand and revenue planning
  • Pilot customers in tech, retail, marketplaces, or logistics
Phase 2

Policy Lab

Add richer observational causal workflows, controlled experiment analysis, and stronger scenario planning for finance, growth, and operating teams.

  • DML-driven policy templates
  • Experiment and quasi-experiment analysis
  • Executive scenario workflows
  • Broader measurement and budget allocation questions
Phase 3

Decision layer

Move toward monitored recommendation systems, adaptive retraining, action logs, and human-governed autonomous analytics.

  • Recommendation engine with approval workflows
  • Decision memory and monitored policy rollouts
  • Stronger enterprise integration
  • Expansion to additional verticals and higher-stakes use cases

Why this sequencing matters

The temptation is to launch as a general AI platform. That is usually a mistake. The better path is to dominate one painful, measurable business problem first.

Commercial clarity

Forecasting plus pricing or promotion intelligence gives a buyer, a budget owner, and an ROI story early.

Technical credibility

It lets the company demonstrate that advanced forecasting and causal methods improve real business decisions, not just technical metrics.

Strategic expansion

Once the wedge works, the same scientific core can extend into marketing measurement, FP&A, operations, transportation, and later public-sector or health applications.

Initial target customers: VP Finance, FP&A leaders, pricing teams, demand planning teams, revenue operations, and measurement or growth science leads in U.S. enterprises.
